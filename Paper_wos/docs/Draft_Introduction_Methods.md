## Detailed introduction outline

### Paragraph 1: The broader methodological shift

**Purpose.** Establish why experimental methods matter across the social sciences and why their diffusion is an important object of study.

**Core claim.** Experimental designs have become increasingly prominent because they permit researchers to manipulate theoretically relevant conditions and strengthen causal inference about attitudes, decisions and behavior. Their use now extends beyond laboratory settings to survey, field, audit, correspondence, conjoint, vignette, list and online designs.

**Evidence needed.** Foundational sources on causal inference and experimental design, plus one or two cross-disciplinary sources documenting the expansion of experimental research. Avoid implying that every design offers the same inferential leverage.

### Paragraph 2: Substantive opportunities created by experimental designs

**Purpose.** Move from the general methodological shift to the kinds of questions these designs make possible.

**Core claim.** Experimental methods allow social scientists to study mechanisms that are difficult to identify with observational data alone, including discrimination, political behavior, institutional legitimacy, preferences, cooperation and responses to public policy.

**Illustrative example.** Audit and correspondence studies of labor-market discrimination provide a clear example because the design can hold applicant qualifications constant while varying socially meaningful characteristics. Add one concise example relevant to Latin American research rather than a long catalogue of methods.

**Evidence needed.** A small number of authoritative reviews or meta-analyses. The paragraph should demonstrate substantive value, not become a methods taxonomy.

### Paragraph 3: Scientific production is globally uneven

**Purpose.** Introduce the sociology-of-science problem that motivates the regional analysis.

**Core claim.** Access to funding, research infrastructure, graduate training, international networks, high-visibility journals and methodological expertise is unevenly distributed across the global scientific system. Consequently, the expansion of a method cannot be assumed to occur uniformly across countries or disciplines.

**Theoretical bridge.** Connect experimental-method production to work on academic dependency, center-periphery relations and the global division of academic labor.

**Evidence needed.** Sources on knowledge production in the Global South and Latin America, including Alatas, Connell, Beigel, Kreimer and Collyer, after verifying the exact works to cite.

### Paragraph 4: Why Latin American affiliation is the primary definition

**Purpose.** Define what the paper means by research "produced in Latin America."

**Core claim.** The main corpus is defined by institutional participation: a paper belongs to the Latin America-affiliated corpus when at least one author reports an affiliation in one of the Latin American countries included in the search. This definition captures participation in knowledge production rather than merely the geographic subject of a study.

**Important distinction.** Research produced by Latin American-affiliated scholars and research about Latin America are related but different phenomena. A study may examine a Latin American country without including any scholar affiliated with the region, while an affiliated scholar may publish research not substantively focused on Latin America.

**Supplementary analysis.** State that the second phenomenon is examined separately in the appendix through a title-, abstract- and keyword-based search, with appropriate validation and limitations.

### Paragraph 5: The empirical gap

**Purpose.** Explain what existing reviews do not yet show.

**Core claim.** Reviews of experimental methods commonly focus on a particular design, discipline, substantive field or selected set of journals. They therefore do not provide a cross-disciplinary account of how Latin America-affiliated experimental research has evolved, which countries and disciplines contribute to it, how internationally collaborative it is, who occupies visible authorship positions, or what substantive themes it addresses.

**Evidence needed.** Identify the closest bibliometric and methodological reviews and describe their scope precisely. Do not claim that no prior work exists until the literature search confirms this.

### Paragraph 6: Research objective and questions

**Purpose.** State the paper's analytical objective without presenting results prematurely.

**Proposed objective.** This paper maps the production of experimental-method research involving Latin American institutional affiliations between 1995 and 2025 and situates it relative to broader patterns in the United States and, once the query is validated, Europe.

**Proposed questions.**

1. How has Latin America-affiliated experimental-method production changed over time, and how does it vary across countries, disciplines and experimental-design families?
2. How is this production organized in terms of authorship, leadership and international collaboration?
3. Which substantive topics characterize the corpus, and how are these topics distributed across disciplines and time?

### Paragraph 7: Contributions

**Purpose.** End the introduction with a compact statement of value.

**Contribution 1.** A transparent, reproducible and cross-disciplinary map of Latin America-affiliated experimental research in WoS.

**Contribution 2.** A distinction between regional participation in knowledge production and the use of Latin America as a substantive research setting.

**Contribution 3.** Evidence on disciplinary concentration, experimental-design families, collaboration, leadership and substantive topics.

**Contribution 4.** A framework that combines bibliometric description with probabilistic text analysis while preserving document-level traceability and manual validation.

**Final sentence.** Briefly preview the paper's organization after the final journal is selected and its preferred article structure is confirmed.

## Data and methods

### Research design

We conducted a descriptive, cross-disciplinary bibliometric study of social-science publications using experimental methods. The study was designed as a rule-based census of records indexed in the Web of Science Core Collection that met the declared search criteria, rather than as a random sample of the literature. The paper is therefore intended to characterize the retrieved WoS universe and not all experimental research conducted in or by scholars from Latin America. The unit of analysis is a unique publication record, identified by its WoS accession number (UT).

The main analysis concerns the origin of scientific production. We define a Latin America-affiliated paper as a record with at least one author reporting an institutional address in one of the 14 Latin American countries retained in the search. This definition differs from a substantive focus on Latin America. Papers that mention the region or one of its countries in their title, abstract or author keywords, but have no Latin American institutional affiliation, are treated as a separate supplementary object of analysis.

### Data source, period and document types

Records were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. The initial searches covered publications dated from January 1, 1990, through July 1, 2026, and were restricted to articles and early-access items. The official curated database retains the partial 2026 records, but the main analyses cover 1995-2025. No eligible Latin America-affiliated records were observed in 1990-1994, and 2026 was excluded from the main period because it contains only a partial year. Exploratory 2026 results are reserved for the appendix.

The search covered WoS subject categories in economics, business and management, political science, international relations, public administration, sociology, psychology and a broader group of social-science fields including education, communication, urban studies, family studies, development studies, demography and interdisciplinary social sciences. The complete category query is reported verbatim in the supplementary materials.

The Emerging Sources Citation Index (ESCI) was launched in 2015 and subsequently added backfiles for earlier years. Because ESCI expanded the representation of regional and emerging journals in the Core Collection, changes over time may partly reflect changes in database coverage rather than publication behavior alone. We retain the WoS index field for every record and plan a robustness analysis that re-estimates the main results after excluding ESCI records.

### Experimental-method search

The base topic query searched titles, abstracts and keywords for explicit names of experimental designs. It included variants of field experiments, survey experiments, audit studies, correspondence experiments and audits, conjoint experiments and analyses, list experiments, factorial experiments and surveys, vignette experiments and surveys, resume or CV audits, choice experiments and online experiments. This query was combined with the selected WoS social-science categories. The unrestricted base search returned 26,446 records before applying the regional retrieval routes. The full syntax, including wildcards and spelling variants, is reproduced in Table 1 or the supplementary materials.

The search deliberately relied on named experimental designs rather than the isolated word "experiment." This choice prioritizes specificity and reproducibility but may miss papers that report an eligible design without using one of the selected expressions. Natural experiments and quasi-experimental studies were not intended to define the corpus because the paper focuses on research involving an explicit experimental manipulation or assignment. Borderline cases are subject to manual review.

### Two retrieval routes

We used two complementary retrieval routes to maximize recall and to preserve the distinction between production and substantive focus.

First, the affiliation-based route combined the experimental-method and subject-category query with the WoS country field (CU) for Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, the Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Guatemala, Mexico, Peru, Uruguay and Venezuela. The original TXT exports contain 716 unique records. This route was formerly labeled "endogenous" in internal files; in the paper it is described only as the affiliation-based search.

Second, the Latin America-focus route combined the same experimental-method and disciplinary criteria with country and regional terms in titles (TI), abstracts (AB) and author keywords (AK). It included country names and demonyms, as well as expressions such as Latin America, South America, Central America, Latin America and the Caribbean, and LAC. The original exports contain 839 unique records. This route was formerly labeled "laboratory" in internal files. We avoid that label because a textual country mention does not establish that Latin America was the experimental site or population.

The two retrieval routes overlapped substantially. After matching on UT, 418 records appeared in both searches, producing 1,137 unique candidate papers. The retrieval-source indicators were used only to audit coverage and were not retained as mutually exclusive analytical categories.

### Geographic scope and country threshold

For the Latin American query, we retained countries with at least 500 WoS publications during the study period. This rule produced the 14-country list used above. Panama, Honduras, Paraguay, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Haiti, Guyana and Suriname were excluded because their recorded production fell below that threshold in the preliminary search. The threshold is a search-scope decision and does not imply that research from excluded countries is substantively unimportant. It limits the claims of the paper to the countries covered by the query and must be acknowledged as a source of undercoverage.

The regional benchmark uses analogous WoS searches for the United States and geographic Europe, applying the same disciplinary, document-type and temporal restrictions. The European search was also designed to retain countries with at least 500 WoS publications during the study period. However, the European country list and resulting experimental and general-production counts are still being validated. European estimates will not be reported as final until the same query definition has been reproduced and checked against the exported TXT files.

### Record reconciliation, screening and manual correction

The two TXT collections were converted from WoS plain text and merged using UT as the unique identifier. When a paper appeared in both retrieval routes, it was retained once. Manual review data were then integrated from the curated workbook. This review supplied missing abstracts, keywords and author-level affiliation information where available and documented inclusion or exclusion decisions.

Forty-eight unique candidate records were excluded on documented grounds, including ineligible subject matter, reviews, duplicate publication versions and records that did not correspond to the intended experimental social-science scope. After these exclusions, 1,089 unique candidates remained.

We then applied the affiliation criterion to the reconciled candidates. Countries were extracted from the final component of each C1 institutional address and matched against the 14-country Latin American dictionary. Extracting the terminal country element, rather than searching for country strings anywhere in the address, prevented false positives such as interpreting New Mexico, USA, as Mexico. A record was retained when at least one validated address belonged to one of the included Latin American countries. This procedure produced an official corpus of 675 unique papers. Of these, 674 had been recovered by the affiliation-based route; one additional record had been recovered by the focus-based route and was retained after manual correction confirmed an Argentine affiliation.

All 675 official records have a nonmissing abstract and at least one usable keyword source after curation. Author keywords were prioritized when present. If the original author-keyword field was missing, manually recovered author keywords were used; WoS-generated keywords were used only when no author keywords were available. The analytical database preserves the original and manually corrected fields separately to maintain traceability.

### Analytical period and regional denominators

The main 1995-2025 corpus contains 611 unique Latin America-affiliated papers. The remaining 64 records were published in partial-year 2026 and are excluded from the main tables, figures and topic model.

To compare regions of different scientific size, we calculate the number of experimental-method papers per 1,000 social-science papers. Denominator searches use the same WoS subject categories, country definitions, document types and years as the experimental searches but omit the experimental-method terms. Absolute experimental and general-production counts are reported alongside relative rates so that a high rate based on a small publication system is not interpreted as a larger volume of production.

We use whole counting. A unique paper contributes once to a region-year total when it includes at least one affiliation in that region. Papers with affiliations in more than one region may therefore contribute to more than one regional series; regional totals are not mutually exclusive and should not be added together. Country and discipline counts follow the same multiple-membership logic. For discipline-specific Latin American denominators assembled from country-level WoS exports, internationally coauthored papers involving more than one included country may be counted in more than one country total. We disclose this overlap rather than treating the summed country counts as unique papers.

The current validated 1995-2025 files contain 611 experimental papers and 155,258 social-science papers for the Latin American query, and 10,970 experimental papers and 1,447,296 social-science papers for the United States. European values remain pending query validation.

### Disciplinary classification

WoS categories were mapped into six core disciplinary groups: business/management, economics, political science, psychology, sociology and other social sciences. Political science includes Political Science, International Relations and Public Administration. The psychology group combines the psychology categories specified in the query. The residual social-science group includes fields such as education, communication, urban studies, family studies, development studies and demography.

Disciplinary membership is not mutually exclusive. A paper indexed in categories mapped to more than one core discipline contributes to each applicable group. All original WoS categories are retained, and any category without an explicit mapping is assigned to Other while being flagged for audit. Consequently, discipline percentages need not sum to 100%.

### Experimental-design classification

We classified the experimental designs described in the title, abstract and curated keyword field using a documented dictionary of regular expressions. The dictionary distinguishes field experiments, lab-in-the-field experiments, laboratory experiments, experimental games, controlled experiments, randomized field interventions, survey experiments, audit studies, correspondence experiments or audits, conjoint designs, list experiments, factorial experiments or surveys, vignette experiments or surveys, resume or CV audits, choice experiments and online experiments. A paper may receive more than one method label.

For presentation, specific methods are grouped into four design families: survey-based experiments; field/lab interventions; conjoint/choice designs; and audit/correspondence designs. Generic labels are retained only when no more informative design is detected. A small set of papers whose abstracts clearly described a design without using a sufficiently stable expression was assigned manually with documented evidence. Papers not mapped to a family are retained as Other/not classified and are undergoing targeted manual review before Table 5 and Figure 4 are frozen.

### Affiliations, collaboration and leadership

Author names and affiliations were reconstructed at the author-paper level using full author names when available and the author-address links encoded in WoS C1 records. Manually recovered C1 and C3 information was linked by author position without overwriting the original fields. Authors may have more than one affiliation, and a paper may include authors from several countries and regions.

International collaboration is classified from the full set of author affiliations. For descriptive figures, papers are assigned to mutually exclusive collaboration categories: Latin America only, Latin America-Europe, Latin America-United States, Latin America-Europe and United States, or Latin America-other. This mutually exclusive collaboration variable differs from the non-mutually-exclusive regional production counts described above.

Leadership in the main analysis is operationalized using the first author's affiliation. A supplementary comparison evaluates first- and corresponding-author affiliation separately because corresponding-author information is obtained from WoS reprint and address fields and may have uneven historical coverage. Multiple affiliations are preserved in the underlying author-level data.

### Topic modeling

We used latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA) to identify recurring substantive vocabularies in the 611-paper analytical corpus (Blei, Ng, and Jordan 2003; Griffiths and Steyvers 2004). LDA represents each paper as a mixture of latent topics and each topic as a probability distribution over terms. Topics are therefore statistical patterns of word co-occurrence rather than pre-existing disciplines or definitive theoretical categories.

The primary text for each paper combines its title and abstract. A sensitivity model adds the curated keyword field. Text was converted to lowercase, normalized and tokenized. Standard English stopwords, connectors, generic academic language, methodological terms and geographic terms introduced by the search design were removed using an auditable dictionary. We inspected corpus-specific bigrams and trigrams before removing ambiguous single words. Substantively meaningful multiword expressions, such as willingness to pay, public policy, vote buying and labor market, were protected and represented as single tokens. Singular and plural variants and selected lexical variants were normalized. Terms appearing in fewer than three papers were removed; frequent terms were not eliminated solely because they occurred in a high proportion of documents.

The resulting primary document-term matrix contains all 611 papers and 2,633 terms. Eight papers were flagged because fewer than 20 retained tokens or 15 unique terms remained after preprocessing, but they were not excluded. This preserves corpus coverage while identifying cases that may have less stable topic assignments.

We estimated LDA models with collapsed Gibbs sampling. Candidate solutions ranged from four to ten topics. For model selection, the corpus was divided once into an 80% training set (488 papers) and a 20% validation set (123 papers), using a fixed random seed. Each candidate value of k was estimated with five initialization seeds. Candidate chains used 500 burn-in iterations, 1,000 sampling iterations and thinning of 100, with symmetric document-topic and topic-term priors of alpha = 0.10 and delta = 0.10.

We evaluated candidate solutions using four complementary criteria: held-out perplexity, UMass semantic coherence, term exclusivity and stability across random initializations. Perplexity assesses out-of-sample predictive fit, while coherence evaluates whether highly probable terms tend to occur in the same documents (Mimno et al. 2011). Exclusivity captures whether top terms distinguish one topic from the others. Stability was measured by optimally matching topics across seeds and calculating cosine similarity between their term-probability distributions, together with agreement in dominant-topic assignments. Each criterion was converted into a rank; perplexity was minimized and coherence, exclusivity and stability were maximized. The ranks were summed, with the smaller k preferred in the event of a tie. This procedure selected eight topics.

The final eight-topic model was re-estimated on all 611 papers with five seeds, using 1,500 burn-in iterations, 3,000 sampling iterations and thinning of 100. We retained the most central run, defined as the seed with the highest average matched-topic similarity to the other runs; the selected seed was 2202. The title-plus-abstract model is the primary specification. A second eight-topic model using titles, abstracts and keywords evaluates sensitivity to the inclusion of keywords by matching topics across the two specifications.

For interpretation, beta denotes the probability of a term conditional on a topic, and gamma denotes the proportion of a paper associated with each topic. The primary comparisons over time and across disciplines use mean gamma, thereby retaining each paper's mixed membership. Counts based on the paper's largest gamma, or dominant topic, are reported as a complementary simplification rather than as the only classification.

Topic labels are not assigned automatically. For each topic, manual validation includes the paper with the highest dominant-topic gamma and the two papers forming the most textually distant pair among papers assigned to that topic. The distant pair is identified using cosine distance between TF-IDF document vectors. Reviewers assess whether these three papers share a defensible substantive theme and propose a concise label. The final topic names and interpretation will be frozen only after this review is completed.

### Reproducibility and limitations of the design

All transformations are implemented in version-controlled R scripts. The workflow rebuilds the official database from the original WoS TXT exports, integrates manual corrections without overwriting the original bibliographic fields, checks expected record counts and unique identifiers at each stage, and exports the document-level data underlying every table and figure.

The design has four main limitations. First, WoS does not represent the entire regional publication system and may underrepresent Spanish- and Portuguese-language outlets. Second, the country threshold excludes low-volume national systems from the main query. Third, affiliation is an imperfect proxy for where knowledge is produced, particularly for mobile scholars and authors with multiple affiliations. Fourth, automated dictionaries and topic models require substantive validation. These limitations are addressed through transparent search syntax, preservation of overlapping affiliations and disciplines, targeted manual review, ESCI sensitivity analyses and supplementary reporting of research focused on Latin America without Latin American affiliations.
