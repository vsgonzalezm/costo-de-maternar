In this paper, our goal is to examine the academic production in the social sciences that uses experimental methods and is produced in Latin America, that is, by authors affiliated with Latin American institutions. We benchmark this production against the U.S. and Europe, examine how it has changed over time (1990–2025), how it varies by discipline, country and type of experimental design, who leads and with whom it is produced, and what the main substantive topics are. We broadly define social sciences as xxx and examine these patterns by core disciplines: political science, economics, sociology, psychology, business/administration, and other social sciences (e.g., education, communication).

Research questions (revise after finishing the theoretical framework):

RQ1. How has the production of experimental research by Latin American-affiliated authors evolved between 1990 and 2025, relative to the U.S. and Europe, and how does it vary across disciplines, countries and types of experimental design?

RQ2. How is this production organized in terms of authorship and collaboration (leadership by first or corresponding authors, collaboration with U.S. and European institutions), and which substantive topics does it address?

This paper allows us to quantify and make visible the use of experimental methods in the social sciences in Latin America, which is key to understanding the disciplines that have moved this forward, the ones that are lagging behind, and the areas where there is much potential to examine the utility of these methods.

Discussion and conclusion

  • Main findings in relation to the research questions and to the sociology of science framework.
  • Strengths and weaknesses of experimental methods in Latin America, based on this review.
  • Limitations:
    • Database coverage: WoS only; Spanish- and Portuguese-language journals and regional journals may be underrepresented; changes in coverage over time (inclusion of ESCI), addressed with the robustness analysis in the appendix.
    • Publications are not the same as the use of the method (unpublished experiments, publication bias) and depend on how papers are tagged by the database.
    • Affiliation as a proxy of production: multiple affiliations, mobility of authors, and possible incomplete address fields in earlier records (report n).
    • Automated classification of disciplines, methods and topics, and its validation.
    • The focus-on-Latin-America search is based on mentions in the title, abstract or keywords and does not identify the study site.
