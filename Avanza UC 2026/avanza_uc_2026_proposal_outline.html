PART 2 — SECTION OUTLINES

a. Executive Summary (1 page) — outline

  1. Hook / problem (2–3 sentences). Chile debates discrimination intensely — migration wave, gender gaps, class and ethnoracial hierarchies, Ley 20.609 — yet virtually all national evidence is perceptual (surveys, complaints). No systematic causal evidence exists on how much discrimination actually occurs, against whom, and under what conditions.
  2. The project (1 short paragraph). Create the first experimental infrastructure in Chile producing causal evidence on discriminatory behavior.
  3. The 18-month deliverables (concrete). (a) Validated Chilean signal bank (names, photos, CVs signaling class, migrant origin, ethnicity, gender) — a methodological public good; (b) a flagship study: a labor-market correspondence study; (c) open protocol + data repository; (d) submission of at least one WoS paper and one large external grant application.
  4. High risk / high reward framing. The open scientific problem: no validated way to signal race/class/migrant status in the Chilean context — genuine methodological frontier, exportable to Latin America and the Global South.
  5. International collaboration. Established co-authors Weisshaar (Northwestern) & Chavez (Indiana) as founding partners with formal commitment and in-kind co-financing (advisory time, comparative US data); ASR 2024 track record.
  6. Seed logic. Explicit runway: Avanza → Fondecyt Regular 2028 + international bid (see Part 4), positioning the lab as a Núcleo Milenio–scale candidate.

b. Hypothesis (½ page) — structure

Frame as one central hypothesis + 2 sub-hypotheses (reviewers reward clarity here — 20% criterion): - H1 (behavior): Discriminatory treatment in the Chilean labor market varies systematically by migrant origin, class signals, and gender. - Methodological conjecture: Class, gender, and ethnoracial categories in Chile can be validly signaled through locally calibrated cues (names, communes, schools, phenotype), enabling a replicable Latin American audit and survey experiment paradigm. - One sentence on why this is risky: signals may fail validation; effects may be null — both outcomes are scientifically publishable (say this explicitly).

c. Methodology (5 pages) — skeleton

  1. Design overview (½ p). To complete.
  2. Phase 1 — Signal development & validation (1–1.5 p). Signal pretests (perceived class, origin, ethnicity ratings); sample; validation criteria; pre-registration.
  3. Phase 2 — Flagship field experiment (1.5 p). Correspondence study in the labor market (build directly on Weisshaar–Chavez–Hutt ASR designs, adapted): sampling frame, factorial design (origin × class × gender), outcomes (callbacks, response quality), power analysis, ethics/deception protocol.
  4. Open science & infrastructure (½ p). Pre-registration, open data/materials repository, replicable protocol = the “lab” as durable output.
  5. Risk management table (¼ p). Signal validation fails → pivot options; low callback rates → sample expansion; ethics delays → conjoint-first sequencing.

d. National relevance and potential impact (1 page)

  • Policy hooks: Ley 20.609 reform debates, migration policy, labor inclusion agendas, INDH mandates.
  • Evidence gap: perception data cannot guide anti-discrimination policy design or evaluation; causal baselines can.
  • Concrete users: ministries (Trabajo, Desarrollo Social), INDH, municipalities, firms (DEI auditing).
  • Institutional impact for UC: first lab of its kind in Chile; teaching/training spillovers (this feeds the “formación de personas” criterion).

e. Global transferability and international collaboration (1 page)

  • The Chilean signal-validation paradigm as an exportable template for Latin America/Global South (most audit methodology is US/EU-calibrated — genuine contribution).
  • Partners: Weisshaar & Chavez — specific roles (design review, comparative US benchmark data, co-authorship on methods + comparative papers), formal cartas de compromiso with valorized in-kind contributions (their institutions’ time/data). Consider adding one LatAm partner (e.g., a Peruvian/Colombian audit-study author) to strengthen “quality of institutions” sub-criterion and regional scaling story.

f. Committed results (1 page) — commit conservatively, deliver more

  • 1 WoS submission (methods/validation paper or flagship empirical paper).
  • 1 application to a larger external competitive fund with annual budget > Avanza (required — see Part 4; Fondecyt Regular is the safe anchor).
  • Public goods: validated signal bank + open protocol + data repository (named, citable).
  • Talent: 2–3 trained RAs; note you cannot fund theses/students — frame as research-skills training via service contracts.
  • 1 policy-facing product (policy brief / public seminar with INDH or ministry).

g. Annual work plan with mid-term evaluation (2 pages)

  • Months 1–4 (Apr–Jul 2027): ethics finalization (certificate already in hand Apr 9), RA hiring, signal pretest fielding, partner kickoff meeting.
  • Months 5–9: signal validation complete + pre-registration; correspondence study fielding begins.
  • Mid-term evaluation (~month 9, Dec 2027): explicit go/no-go milestones — validated signals, ≥50% of audit sample fielded, conjoint pre-registered. Include a contingency branch.
  • Months 10–14: complete fielding; analysis.
  • Months 15–18 (to Oct 2028): papers drafted/submitted; external grant application submitted; public launch event of the lab; data repository release. (Remember: ≤10% of budget in final quarter — front-load spending.)
